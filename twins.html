Two accounts, two markets, one journey

Opening both a Hong Kong and a mainland China account in a single onboarding flow, designed to live inside WeChat, the app Chinese residents already use every day, and delivered bilingually for a cross-border audience.

My roleSenior Product UX Design Manager: Origination
MarketsHong Kong & mainland China
SurfaceWeChat-embedded journey · EN / 中文
The challenge

Two banking systems, two regulators, one customer.

Cross-border customers needed accounts on both sides of the border, but Hong Kong and mainland China have different eligibility rules, identity requirements, and account-usage regulations. Traditionally that meant two separate applications, twice the friction.

The goal: collapse it into one continuous journey that opens both accounts, meets each market's rules, and meets the customer where they already are, inside WeChat, rather than a separate app or branch.

Approach

Three decisions shaped the journey.

1Where it lives

Embed in WeChat

Meet Chinese residents in their daily super-app instead of asking them to download or visit anything new.

2One flow, two markets

Merge the applications

Sequence HK and China steps into a single path, branching only where each regulator requires it.

3Built bilingually

English & 中文 in parallel

Every screen designed in both languages so the experience is native for either audience.

Journey design

Mapping the whole cross-border flow.

I designed the end-to-end journey across both markets: from the shared entry point through identity, personal and employment detail, market-specific account usage (China and HK), eligibility, appointment booking, and confirmation. Branches handle the points where the two regulators diverge.

The complete service blueprint: shared entry point, then branching HK / China paths, reconverging at confirmation.
The flow

From entry to two open accounts.

Key steps as the customer experiences them in the WeChat-embedded journey.

Bilingual by design

Native in English and 中文.

Rather than translate after the fact, each screen was designed in both languages in parallel, so layout, labels, and form density hold up whether the customer reads English or Chinese. This is what lets one journey serve a genuinely cross-border audience.

Outcome

One journey where there used to be two.
